# Agricultural checks, selected by commodity and region

This is a research checklist, not a sourced production forecast or a universal crop calendar. Record the applicable choices and evidence in each specification. An unmeasured condition is a limitation, not an estimated value.

| Area | Required distinctions and checks |
|---|---|
| Crop/marketing year | Map each observation to its crop, region/hemisphere, production year and marketing year. Identify planting, flowering/filling and harvest windows from region-specific primary evidence. South American and US soybean stages cannot share one calendar. Record leap-week/week-53 treatment if weekly seasons are added. |
| Weather exposure | Use crop-region area/exposure weights published and available at the decision time. Preserve both weather forecast issuance/target and observed weather dates. Do not use later realized acreage to weight old weather or convert rainfall mechanically to national yield. |
| Lunar New Year | Separate holiday production shutdowns, logistics disruption and anticipatory demand. Compare holiday-relative windows where justified. v1 implements only an explicitly selected calendar-month historical anomaly; it does not implement lunar or agronomic alignment. Changing seasonal alignment requires a reviewed operator and tests. |
| Imports | Preserve overseas output → export sales/commitments → loading → arrival → customs clearance → locally usable stock as separate stages. Reconcile periods, cancelations, transport lags, tariffs, currency/FX vintage, tonne/bushel conversion, moisture/protein/quality, and delivery location. A vessel arrival is not automatically available crushing feedstock. |
| Oilseed crushing | Distinguish domestic food-use soybeans and imported crushing feedstock. Treat meal and oil as joint outputs with separate end-demand and inventory constraints. Document plant/feedstock/time-specific yields, losses and byproducts; never hard-code universal meal/oil extraction rates. Margin inputs require prices on a consistent tax, currency, date, quality and location basis and a documented physical conversion. |
| Feed and livestock | Head counts, animal weights, age/growth stages, productivity and feed intensity are distinct. Preserve breeding-to-output and replenishment lags. Substitution between meals/grains needs nutritional, technical and species constraints; price ratios alone do not establish feasible substitution. |
| Product biology | Grain/oil storage and inventory carry do not transfer directly to livestock/eggs, fruit, sugar or cotton. Check perishability, cold storage, live-weight cycles, processing recovery, delivery grades and end uses for each family. Do not inherit a soybean demand or storage assumption merely by changing the symbol. |
| Inventory coverage | Distinguish port, processor, downstream, exchange-registered and state-reserve stocks. Record owner/location/sample membership and coverage changes. Reconcile transfers; never add overlapping stocks. Warehouse receipts are deliverable registered inventory, not total supply. Coverage denominators need matched geography, sample and units; sales, shipments, consumption and crush are not interchangeable. |
| Policy/delivery events | Record announcement, effective date, auction offer, actual sale, payment, delivery and physical availability separately. Offered volume is not a completed sale. Delivery changes and receipt expiry can move inventories without changing total supply. |

Keep **physical state/forecast** (for example cleared arrivals in tonnes) separate from **market context** (price action, positioning, sentiment). Margins, basis, spreads, FX-sensitive landed costs and any price-dependent transformations must be tagged. Do not lower a supply forecast simply because sentiment is bullish or price rose. If the price response invalidates a mechanism, record the counterevidence and propose a separately reviewed specification.
