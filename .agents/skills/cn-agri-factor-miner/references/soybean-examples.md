# Three illustrative hypotheses — unvalidated

Executable specs live in `examples/specifications.json` and individual `examples/spec-*.json`. All observations are fixed-seed **synthetic** fixtures. They demonstrate mechanics only. No IC, returns, significance or alpha evidence exists.

The baseline catalog provides transparent equations, not allegedly validated published factors. New research must inspect its actual existing-factor library before adopting these names or baselines. The linked soybean meal/oil group is configurable; other agricultural groups need their own metadata, data rules and economic assumptions.

| Hypothesis | Formula and baseline | Required data | Mechanism / counterexample |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seasonally abnormal meal inventory coverage | `-(meal_stock / weekly_meal_disappearance - training-only same-month historical coverage)`; baseline is raw inventory coverage. Positive means tighter coverage. | Same-sample processor stock and weekly disappearance in tonnes and tonnes/week; at least two prior eligible years; actual publication vintages; sample coverage; calendar/Lunar New Year controls. | Low coverage may precede delivery tightness. It may instead reflect sample exits, transfers downstream or collapsing demand. Test next released physical stocks/disappearance, not merely subsequent price. Calendar-month normalization is a toy choice requiring agricultural review. |
| Dated arrival balance relative to stocks and crush | `-(cleared_arrivals_forecast_next14d + usable_raw_stock - planned_crush_next14d) / planned_crush_next14d`; baseline is stocks minus planned use. | Dated forecast for the exact next 14-day interval, customs/quality-cleared locally usable arrivals, raw stock and crush plan on matching location/sample/tonne basis, subsequent actual arrivals/crush for evaluation. | Feedstock shortages may constrain joint product supply. Port congestion, clearance failures, canceled plans or a faster demand decline invalidate the chain. This is a balance forecast; a revision is not a surprise. |
| Margin change conditional on physical constraints | `-Δ7d_margin × min(1, raw_stock/planned_crush14d) × min(1/(1+meal_coverage/4 weeks), 1/(1+oil_coverage/6 weeks))`; baseline is raw margin change. | Consistently priced feedstock-equivalent margin, raw stock/crush plans, separate meal/oil stocks and disappearance, maintenance/logistics evidence; plant/feedstock-specific yields if calculating margin upstream. | Margin improvement may increase crush only with feedstock and outlets for both joint products. A demand-driven margin jump or plant shutdown can reverse the return sign. Gate scales 4/6 weeks are illustrative predeclared assumptions, not estimated optimal levels. Test actual crush and both product inventories. |

All three require independent external evaluation. Return targets are meal only for the first hypothesis and separately evaluated meal/oil for the linked supply hypotheses; the two products are not enough for a robust cross-sectional IC design. An apparently plausible sign is not predictive evidence. Keep output volume forecasts separate from sentiment/price-context commentary; the third factor is explicitly price-derived.

The first example's original seasonal history minimum is two years. The simulated revise/resume demonstration changes this to three, retains version 1, invalidates its approval and consumes one substantive revision slot. Simulated reviewers and synthetic success never populate the approved research library.
